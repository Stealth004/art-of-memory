# Notes — Modern science of learning & memory (read 2026-10-02, index)

1. Ebbinghaus, H. (1885). Über das Gedächtnis; Eng. trans. Memory: A Contribution to Experimental Psychology (1913, Ruger & Bussenius). Self-experiments with ~2,300 nonsense syllables; forgetting curve: rapid early loss then flattening; "savings" method of relearning. Verified via yorku.ca Classics in the History of Psychology (memory3.htm, memory8.htm); simple.wikipedia.org; taalhammer.com. Quote (ch. 8, §31): "the series which have been learned twice fade away much more slowly than those which have been learned but once. If the re-learning is performed a second, a third or a greater number of times, the series are more deeply engraved and fade out less easily."

2. Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354–380. Meta-analysis: 839 assessments of distributed practice in 317 experiments (184 articles). Key: optimal inter-study interval grows with retention interval; expanding intervals examined. (yorku.ca/ncepeda; nature.com nrn.2015.18 refs.)

3. Atkinson, R. C., & Shiffrin, R. M. (1968). Modal/multi-store model: sensory → short-term → long-term stores; control processes (attention, rehearsal, coding, retrieval). (db.arabpsychology.com; psychcentral.com; opentext.wsu.edu.)

4. Baddeley, A. D., & Hitch, G. J. (1974). Working memory model: central executive + phonological loop + visuo-spatial sketchpad (episodic buffer added 2000). Dual-task evidence. (en.wikipedia.org Baddeley's model of working memory.)

5. Miller, G. A. (1956). "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two," Psychological Review, 63, 81–97. Chunking: capacity is a fixed number of chunks; bigger chunks carry more information. (github hashgraph-online SKILL.md quoting paper; newsroom.unsw.edu.au.)
   Cowan, N. (2001). "The magical number 4 in short-term memory," Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 24, 87–114. Pure capacity ≈ 4 chunks when rehearsal/chunking blocked. (wixtedlab.ucsd.edu PDF; scirp reference.)

6. Craik, F. I. M., & Lockhart, R. S. (1972). Levels of processing: A framework for memory research. Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behaviour, 11, 671–684. Depth of processing (structural → phonemic → semantic) determines trace durability; elaborative > maintenance rehearsal. (en.wikipedia.org; bcp.psych.ualberta.ca.) Follow-up: Craik & Tulving (1975) elaboration; Rogers et al. (1977) self-reference effect (simplypsychology.org).

7. Tulving, E. (1972). "Episodic and semantic memory," in Tulving & Donaldson (Eds.), Organization of Memory. Episodic = temporally dated personal events; semantic = "mental thesaurus," knowledge of words/symbols/meanings. (ebsco.com; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov PMC2952732.)

8. Paivio, A. (1971). Dual coding theory: two functionally independent but interconnected systems — verbal and imaginal (nonverbal). "Imagery potentiates recall of verbal material because when a word evokes an associated image... two separate but linked memory traces are laid down, one in each of the memory stores." (plato.stanford.edu, Winter 2009 archive.)

9. Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. Psychological Science, 17(3), 249–255. Two experiments, prose passages: at 5 min restudy (SSSS) beat testing 81% vs 75%; at 2 days testing pulled ahead; at 1 week STTT 61% vs SSSS 40%; restudy group read passage ~14 times vs ~3.4 for tested group, yet recalled less. (journals.sagepub.com abstract; source.washu.edu 2006.)

10. Rohrer, D., & Taylor, K. (2007). The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning. Instructional Science, 35(6), 481–498. Exp. 1: spaced > massed at 1-week test. Exp. 2: interleaved (mixed) > blocked practice at 1-week test. Later review (Rohrer 2012): interleaved 63% vs blocked 20%, d = 1.34 (uweb.cas.usf.edu). (eric.ed.gov EJ786797.)

11. Bjork, R. A. (1994). "Memory and metamemory considerations in the training of human beings," in Metcalfe & Shimamura (Eds.), Metacognition: Knowing about knowing, MIT Press, pp. 185–205. Desirable difficulties: conditions impairing short-term performance (spacing, interleaving, retrieval practice, varied practice) enhance long-term retention/transfer. Learning vs performance distinction; Soderstrom & Bjork (2015) review. (taalhammer.com; researchgate.net.)

12. Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning. Cognitive Science, 12, 157–183. Means-ends analysis consumes working memory and interferes with schema acquisition; worked examples superior for novices. (github jmagly REF-006; github noneerroksome CLT summary.)

13. Scoville, W. B., & Milner, B. (1957). Loss of recent memory after bilateral hippocampal lesions. Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery, and Psychiatry, 20, 11–21. Patient H.M.: bilateral medial temporal resection → severe anterograde amnesia with intact short-term memory, old memories, IQ, personality. Hippocampus "critically concerned in the retention of current experience." (pubmed 10678523; mcgill.ca PDF.)

14. Bliss, T. V. P., & Lømo, T. (1973). Long-lasting potentiation of synaptic transmission in the dentate area of the anaesthetized rabbit following stimulation of the perforant path. Journal of Physiology, 232(2), 331–356. First demonstration of LTP: high-frequency stimulation → synaptic efficiency increase lasting 30 min–10 hr; leading cellular model of memory. (physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com abstract; Lømo 2003 Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B retrospective, pubmed 12740104.)

15. Diekelmann, S., & Born, J. (2010). The memory function of sleep. Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 11, 114–126. Sleep (esp. slow-wave sleep) consolidates memory: system consolidation (hippocampal → neocortical transfer/reorganization) plus synaptic consolidation. (nature.com nrn2762-c2 correspondence.)

16. Maguire, E. A., Valentine, E. R., Wilding, J. M., & Kapur, N. (2003). Routes to remembering: The brains behind superior memory. Nature Neuroscience, 6, 90–95. 10 superior memorizers (8 World Memory Championship competitors): 9/10 used method of loci; engaged spatial-memory brain regions (incl. right posterior hippocampus); no advantage in verbal IQ/matrix reasoning; no structural brain differences. (the-scientist.com 2002 report; auntminnie.com 2003; sagepub Caplan et al. 2019 ref list.)

17. Dresler, M., et al. (2017). Mnemonic training reshapes brain networks to support superior memory. Neuron, 93(5), 1227–1235. 23 top memory athletes vs controls; naive subjects randomized to 6 weeks method-of-loci training (~30 min/day), working-memory training, or no training. MoL group: 26 → 62 words of 72 (more than doubled); gains persisted at 4 months; functional connectivity reorganized toward athlete pattern. (mendeley; neurosciencenews.com; sciencenews.org; science.org scitranslmed.aan0770.)

18. Twomey, C., & Kroneisen, M. (2021). The effectiveness of the loci method as a mnemonic device: Meta-analysis. Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology. 13 RCTs: g = 0.65, 95% CI [0.45, 0.85]; robust to bias adjustments but high risk of bias (poor randomization reporting), mostly university samples. (journals.sagepub.com 10.1177/1747021821993457.)
    Ondřej et al. (2025). Systematic review & meta-analysis, British Journal of Psychology: large effect for immediate serial recall vs rehearsal controls, d = 0.88, 95% CI [0.47, 1.25]; evidence graded low quality. (bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com 10.1111/bjop.12799.)
    Caveat summary (github fourjuaneight/wiki): effects are material-specific; no transfer to fluid intelligence/general WM.

19. Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. 10 techniques reviewed: HIGH utility — practice testing, distributed practice; MODERATE — interleaved practice, elaborative interrogation, self-explanation; LOW — rereading, highlighting, summarization, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text. (journals.sagepub.com 10.1177/1529100612453266.)
