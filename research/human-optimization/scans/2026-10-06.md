## Learning & Memory

- **2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine — context event (announced Oct 5; pre-flagged to the user Oct 5 evening; NOT breaking news for the chat report).** Awarded to Karl Deisseroth (Stanford), Peter Hegemann (Humboldt University, Berlin), and Georg Nagel (University of Würzburg) "for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics"; 12M SEK (~$1.2M) shared. Science chain: Hegemann's 1990s Chlamydomonas phototaxis work → Hegemann+Nagel show channelrhodopsin is a light-gated ion-channel switch (early 2000s) → Deisseroth expresses channelrhodopsin in mammalian neurons (2005), light on/off control of defined neuron populations in living brains (mice, by ~2007). Nobel committee: circuits governing "specific memories, feelings, and behaviors relevant for neurological and psychiatric disorders." The memory-engram literature (tagging/reactivating the neurons that hold a specific memory) is built directly on this tool — and the repo already carries an annotated engram-papers index. Clinical status: essentially nil human application (one retinitis-pigmentosa patient with partial vision recovery, 2021; Parkinson's/epilepsy cited as future). Nagel told press he thought it was "still too early" for the medicine prize. **Relevance to this corpus:** optogenetics is the experimental substrate under the scan's Learning & Memory program entries (RAM, TNT target the same plasticity/memory machinery by other means) — but it is an animal-model research tool, not a training intervention, and says nothing about mnemonic efficacy. **Speculation flag:** do not let this feed "super soldier" narratives; clinical human application is essentially nil. Nobel week continues (physics, chemistry, literature, peace, economics) — watch for anything in-domain.

## Athleticism & Physical Performance

- **Pneumatic knee-actuated exoskeleton — NEW TO THE KNOWLEDGE BASE (peer-reviewed).** Johnson, Sarantos, Fohrmann, Hollander, Zeppetelli, Dempsey & Nagai; Military Performance Division, USARIEM (Natick, MA) + MSH Medical School Hamburg. *J Appl Biomech* 42(2):77–84; epub 2025 Dec 15, print 2026 Apr 1; PMID 41468233, DOI 10.1123/jab.2025-0125. Eleven active-duty Army soldiers (9M, age 22±4) completed a 2-mile treadmill ruck march at self-selected pace (1.34±0.10 m/s) carrying 30% bodyweight (+9.07 kg for the EXO in the EXO session). IMU-derived local dynamic stability (LDS, a motor-control measure) over 100 strides at start and end: right-shank LDS significantly lower with EXO vs no-EXO (mean diff 0.28, P<.01, partial η²=.75); left shank same direction, large effect but n.s. (P=.07, η²=.29); pelvis LDS higher with EXO, large effect but n.s. (P=.07, η²=.29). Authors' conclusion: lower-extremity EXOs reduce distal LDS — **habituation periods for new users are needed**. Small n, tightly controlled — the first USARIEM Military Performance Division EXO study in the KB. Logged as enabling/research, not enhancement.
- **SABER back exosuit — backfill (published Dec 2024, *Wearable Technologies*; missed by prior scans).** Vanderbilt (Karl Zelik's lab) with U.S. Army Soldiers; passive elastic, unpowered back exosuit developed for field artillery/logistics sustainment with the 101st Airborne Division (Army Pathfinder funding, 2021–22); commercialized by HeroWear. Repeated heavy-object lifting (45–55 kg) to failure, with vs without exosuit: 88% of participants increased max lifting repetitions; endurance +28–75%; Exo-LiFFT ergonomic model: −27–93% cumulative back damage even with more lifts performed (peak lumbar moment reduced 15–42 Nm). First study to test the dual-benefit question (does lifting more cancel the injury-risk reduction?): it does not. Authors do *not* recommend using most occupational back exos to lift substantially heavier weights. Injury-prevention + endurance aid for occupational lifting — enabling precondition for training, adjacent to H2F, not performance enhancement of the operator.
