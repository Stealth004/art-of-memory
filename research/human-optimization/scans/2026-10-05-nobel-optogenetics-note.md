## Facts (verified the evening of Oct 5, 2026 PDT)

- Announced Monday Oct 5 by the Nobel Assembly at Karolinska Institute: **Karl Deisseroth** (54, American, Stanford — bioengineering & psychiatry), **Peter Hegemann** (71, German), **Georg Nagel** (73, German). Citation: "discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics." Prize sum 12M SEK (~$1.2M), shared.
- Science chain: 1990s — Hegemann studies how the alga Chlamydomonas swims toward light and identifies the light-sensitive protein; 2000s — Hegemann + Nagel show channelrhodopsin is a light-gated ion-channel "switch"; 2005 — Deisseroth expresses channelrhodopsin in mammalian neurons, enabling light on/off control of defined neuron populations in living brains. Nobel committee: "a completely new dimension of understanding of the function of the brain."
- What it enables: mapping which circuits govern specific memories, feelings, and behaviors — explicitly including learning, fear, addiction, and movement. The memory-engram literature (tagging/reactivating the neurons that hold a specific memory) is built directly on this tool.
- Clinical status: very limited human use. One blind patient with retinitis pigmentosa recovered partial vision in a 2021 optogenetic trial; Parkinson's and epilepsy are cited as future possibilities. Nagel himself told press he thought it was "still too early" for the medicine prize given only one patient has benefited.
- Nobel week continues: physics, chemistry, literature, peace, economics announced over the coming days — watch for anything in-domain (learning/memory, neurotech).
