## 1. The iCanStudy system in brief

**Core claim [Sung]:** most study failure is an *encoding* failure, not a retrieval failure. Fix how knowledge is built and retrieval largely takes care of itself. Distinguishes **retention** (how long knowledge stays available) from **mastery** (quality, integration, usability).

### 1.1 Bear Hunter System (BHS) — the encoding engine
1. Identify keywords and terminology.
2. **Restricted inquiry → self-explanation → main chunks:** bounded questions about functional/conceptual importance and relationships between keywords.
3. Restricted inquiry again on relationships *between* chunks; assign **strict relative priority**.
4. Represent ideas and relationships **nonlinearly** (mind-map-like), with illustrations.
5. **Recurse:** repeat; isolate genuinely disconnected elements for spaced retrieval or rote.
- **Reverse thinking:** learn higher-order structure *before* lower-order details. Rote memory is reserved for residual isolated facts only.
- **[Synthesis]:** the *full* procedural steps are not publicly disclosed (paid programme); public material teaches the component principles.

### 1.2 Three Cognitive Pillars
- **Schema Construction:** skim all resources → collect keywords on one page → use prior knowledge → *guess* initial relationships → rough-draft schema only. Do NOT dive into detail.
- **Schema Assimilation:** consume in small units; connect each unit to the existing schema; easy concepts first; add knowledge in layers.
- **Schema Reorganisation:** stop input every ~10–15 min; regroup, simplify, remove, relabel, redraw relationships.

### 1.3 Four Layers of Learning
1. **Logic** — big picture, ~10–15 min for a 1–2-hour topic. *Determines how easily the rest learns.*
2. **Concepts** — exam-level depth. **Spend ~60–70% of total time here.**
3. **Important Details** — concretise Layer-2 concepts. Flashcards appropriate *here*.
4. **Arbitrary Details** — isolated facts; usually ignore; flashcards/rote only if tested.
- Red flag: making flashcards early = starting at Layer 3/4.

### 1.4 LADDER
Marbles (new information) need cups (mental categories); learning = the effortful decision of which cup each marble belongs in. Successive **low-effort rungs**: Rung 1 — only what's easy (~10–15 min scaffold); Rung 2 — what now feels easy, more detail; Rung 3 — remaining details (relational → map, isolated → flashcards). **Never spend time on hard things on a given pass.** Side benefit: studying stops feeling aversive → less procrastination.

### 1.5 Blind Mapping
Map *before* studying: select keywords without mastering them, organise and connect from memory and prior knowledge, **guessing** at relationships; then compare against sources, correct, reorganise, repeat. Balances consumption, digestion, testing.

### 1.6 Mind mapping (3 levels)
- Level 1: nonlinear with connections but messy (training wheels).
- Level 2: 20–30+ seconds of thought *before* writing anything — forces higher-order thinking. Diagnostic: if you can write constantly, you're not thinking hard enough.
- Level 3: clear structure/flow; **visual emphasis** marks importance; groups **relabelled with your own intuitive names**, not textbook terminology. The level-2→3 move is *quality of relationships*.

### 1.7 GRINDE Study Map — the evolved form [User-supplied, 2026-10-05/06]

Not Buzan-style mind mapping (radial association, decoration). GRINDE is a **functional study map**: every element serves encoding, retrieval, or exam logic — targeted by functional, importance, or exam logic rather than free association. The user shared the complete step-by-step framework (10 photos, *"Grinde Study Map — Step-by-step Framework"*); what follows is transcribed from those photos.

*Provenance:* confirmed as Sung's published teaching — his video "The Ultimate Guide to The Perfect Mindmap" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Grd7K7bJVWg) teaches Grouping, Relational, Interconnected, Nonverbal, Directional, Emphasized on camera, including this document's terms ("backbone," "Islands" as the anti-pattern of isolated clusters, "memory landmarks"). The full step-by-step framework here (worksheet, prompt bank, legend, quality bar, spacing schedule) was transcribed from the user's photos of the framework document (2026-10-05/06); the six letters themselves are his.

**The GRINDE prompts** (what to ask yourself while mapping):
- **G — Grouping** (chunk the landscape): What are the natural buckets? (components, processes, roles, risks, controls). "If I had to teach this in 3–5 tiles on a slide, what would each tile be?" *Checklist:* 3–7 groups, each named **verb+noun** (e.g., *Establish Identity*, *Grant Rights*).
- **R — Relational** (make the links visible): How does A affect B? Cause→effect; depends-on; part-of; contrasts-with. Necessary vs sufficient? Trade-offs? *Checklist:* label links (*enables, constrains, verifies, mitigates*).
- **I — Interconnected** (cross-link between groups): Where do groups interact? What flows across them (data, keys, tickets, permissions)? Can I trace one scenario end-to-end? *Checklist:* ≥2 cross-group links per group.
- **N — Nonverbal** (encode with visuals): replace words with icons, arrows, timelines, swimlanes, mini-graphs; **shape = type** (circles=actors, squares=systems, diamonds=decisions). *Checklist:* ≥1 doodle/legend; arrows show direction and labels.
- **D — Directional** (flow, sequence, priority): what is the order (time) or priority (risk→control)? Mark feedback loops, gatekeepers, handoffs. *Checklist:* numbers 1→N or lane diagrams.
- **E — Emphasized** (what actually matters?): box the exam-worthy or design-critical ideas (definitions, thresholds, pitfalls); star the *why* — "We do X to achieve Y; trade-off is Z." *Checklist:* 3–7 emphasis marks (box, star, color) with one-line reasons.

**Step-by-step worksheet** (use each time):
1. **Set the Goal (Prime):** topic + outcome in one line (e.g., *Explain AuthN vs AuthZ with examples*); scope size and timebox (Mini or Standard).
2. **Harvest raw pieces (Prime):** list 5–12 key nouns/verbs from headings, bold terms, figures; note given workflows/tables to reuse as structure.
3. **Make first-pass groups (G):** cluster into 3–7 verb+noun groups; rename until each title tells *function* (*Verify Identity* > "Authentication").
4. **Draw in-group links (R):** 2–3 labeled links per group (*enables / requires / contradicts*); add counter-examples or failure modes.
5. **Add cross-group bridges (I):** arrows crossing groups, labeled with what flows (data/keys/permissions); trace one scenario end-to-end, mark path 1→2→3.
6. **Layer visuals (N + D):** shapes→types + legend; sequence (numbers/swimlanes) + feedback loops.
7. **Emphasize the essentials (E):** box critical definitions, thresholds, pitfalls with why-it-matters notes; star 3–7 high-yield items you'll retrieve later.
8. **Retrieve & Reflect (close the loop):** close material; explain the map to a wall/peer in 90 seconds; answer 2 prompts from memory (Prompt Bank); mark gaps → next session.

**Timing presets:** Mini (25–30 min): Prime 3 / Map 12 / Deepen 8 / Retrieve 5. Standard (45–60 min): Prime 5 / Map 20 / Deepen 15 / Retrieve 10. (Deepen 8–15 min: add connections. Retrieve 3–10 min: close the book; explain, draw from memory, or answer two exam-style prompts; note gaps → next iteration.)

**Visual legend (suggested):** Circle = Actor (user, admin, service); Square = System/Component (IdP, switch, HSM); Parallelogram = Data/Artifact (token, key, ticket); Diamond = Decision/Gate (policy check); Solid arrow = Data/Control flow (label verbs); Dashed arrow = Dependency/assumption; Numbers 1→N for sequence; colored box/star for emphasis.

**Prompt Bank** (to force higher-order thinking): **Mechanism** — exactly how does A cause B? what must be true? **Necessary vs Sufficient** — is X required, sufficient, or both? when not? **Trade-off** — if Y increases, what degrades? latency? security? cost? **Edge case** — what breaks if input is malformed, out-of-order, adversarial? **Compare/Contrast** — why SAML vs OAuth here? **Analogy** — non-tech metaphor in 1–2 lines. **Reverse** — if the outcome failed, which step *upstream likely failed first?*

**Quality Bar (self-check):** every group has purpose-based title + 2+ labeled links; ≥2 cross-group links per group + one numbered end-to-end scenario; nonverbal elements + legend; 3–7 emphasized items with *why-it-matters* notes; you can **recreate the map from memory at 60–80% fidelity in 2–3 minutes**.

**Spacing & interleaving schedule:** Day 0 — build first-pass + one retrieval. Day 1 (5–8 min) — re-draw from memory; add one new edge case. Day 3–4 (5–8 min) — interleave with a related topic (e.g., keys & tokens with PKI). Day 7 (5–8 min) — final retrieval + teach-back.

**One-page checklist (print/duplicate):** goal set & timeboxed; 5–12 raw pieces listed; 3–7 function-named groups; in-group links labeled; cross-group bridges added; visual legend + sequence; 3–7 emphasized items with reasons; 2 retrieval prompts answered from memory.

**Tip:** *"Rough first, pretty later. Thinking beats neatness. If it feels a bit hard, you're doing it right."*

**Worked example (micro, 10-minute sketch) — Authentication vs Authorization:** Groups — Establish Identity (AuthN), Grant Rights (AuthZ), Transport & Tokens, Policies & Roles. Relational — AuthN *enables* AuthZ; tokens *carry* claims *used by* the policy engine to allow/deny. Interconnected — IdP (AuthN) → issues token → resource server evaluates policy → grants or denies. Nonverbal/Directional — swimlanes (User | IdP | App | Policy), numbered steps 1→6, token drawn as a ticket. Emphasized — "AuthN ≠ AuthZ", "Least privilege", "Scope creep risk". Retrieve — *"Why can a valid login still get a 403?"*

**[Synthesis]:** GRINDE is the grown-up form of the Level-3 mind map from the 2026-09 research: Level 3's "intuitive relabelling" became purpose-based group titles; "visual emphasis" became the Emphasized step. The Prompt Bank is restricted inquiry / self-explanation operationalized as seven reusable questions; the closing Retrieve step bakes the Iteration Effect into the map; the Day 0/1/3–4/7 schedule mechanizes spacing + interleaving (Cepeda 2006; Rohrer & Taylor 2007); the Quality Bar's "recreate from memory at 60–80% in 2–3 minutes" is itself a retrieval-practice test. The whole framework is a *procedure for generating* higher-order thinking on demand — which is exactly what Sung's system always claimed to be.

### 1.7 Supporting methods
- **Interleaving:** mix topics/problem types in revision (strongest for confusable problem types) **[Established science: Taylor & Rohrer 2010]**.
- **Effort/Time Exchange:** deliberately increase productive effort early to reduce total relearning later.
- **Iteration Effect:** test early and often to **expose errors, not validate confidence**.
- **Omni-Learner:** reject fixed "learning styles"; organisation beats modality **[Established science: Pashler et al. 2008]**.
- **RAIL** (skill learning): Relevance → Awareness → **Iteration (accuracy and consistency BEFORE speed)** → Lifelong maintenance.
- **Broken Barrel + Five Dimensions:** deep processing, self-regulation, mindset, retrieval, self-management. Diagnose the weakest plank, fix it, re-evaluate every 3–4 months. **[Sung]** reports 70%+ of assessed learners are mindset-limited.
- **14-Step Guide:** Measure → Have (plan) → Priming → Intense sessions → confusion→question → Test (match exam style/cues; feedback only after effort; **revise only after ~20–30% forgetting**; rehearse under matched intensity/nerves/timing).
- **Session rhythm:** consumption–digestion–testing cycle; reorganise roughly every 10–15 min.

### 1.8 Science foundations he teaches
Atkinson–Shiffrin (1968); Ebbinghaus (1885); Sweller's CLT six principles; chunking/schemas (Miller 1956; Chase & Simon 1973); generation effect (Slamecka & Graf 1978); desirable difficulties (Bjork 1994); levels of processing (Craik & Lockhart 1972); testing effect (Roediger & Karpicke 2006); spacing (Cepeda et al. 2006); dual coding (Paivio 1971); interleaving; pretesting effect ("take a guess"); metacognition/SRL (Koriat & Bjork; Zimmerman).

**His contrarian stance [Sung, defensible but his doctrine]:** spaced retrieval is overgeneralised — use it to *augment* an encoding-optimised system, never as the system itself; flashcards only at Layers 3/4. The strong version is pedagogical doctrine, not experimental consensus.

### The central synthesis

**Sung is encoding-first; the classical art is storage-and-retrieval-first.** Sung teaches how to *build* the schema (BHS, Pillars, LADDER, Layers); the art of memory teaches how to *hold and recover* it (ordered loci, striking images, traversable series). They interlock rather than compete:

- **Where Sung is weakest, the art is strongest:** Layer 4 arbitrary details. Sung resorts to rote flashcards here; the method of loci is precisely a technology for arbitrary detail — Dresler et al. 2017: 26→62 words after six weeks of loci training. **Upgrade:** encode Layer-4 facts as *imagines agentes* placed in loci instead of flat flashcards.
- **Where the art is silent, Sung speaks:** the classical sources say *what* to build (places, images) but little about *how* to arrive at the relationships — Sung's restricted inquiry and schema construction fill exactly that gap.
- **Shared enemy:** both systems attack the same failure mode — premature detail-loading into an unstructured mind (Sung's Layer-3/4 red flag = Quintilian's overload warnings = Sweller's cognitive load).

### Honest divergences

1. **Sequencing:** Sung's "encode first, retrieve second" and his inverted-U of repetition are his doctrine — defensible, but the experimental consensus still ranks retrieval practice + spacing as the highest-utility techniques known (Dunlosky et al. 2013).
2. **Status of mnemonics:** Sung ranks classical mnemonics mid-tier and barely uses them; the AoM evidence (Maguire 2003: 9/10 elite memorizers used loci, no IQ advantage) suggests he underrates them for arbitrary-detail retention.
3. **Military overlap:** no military study in the human-optimization scan tests loci or spaced repetition directly — cross-references there are conceptual, not demonstrated.

## 3. Combined workflow (practical)

1. **Prime** (Four Layers L1 + LADDER Rung 1 + Blind Mapping): skim, collect keywords, guess relationships, rough-draft schema. ~10–15 min.
2. **Build the palace** (classical): choose a set of ordered loci matching the topic's structure.
3. **Encode** (BHS + Pillars): restricted inquiry → chunks with strict priority → nonlinear map; convert key ideas into striking *imagines agentes* and place them in the loci.
4. **Reorganise every ~10–15 min:** stop input; redraw/recall the map and walk the loci.
5. **Details:** Layer-3 details into the map; Layer-4 arbitrary facts → loci images (not flat flashcards).
6. **Test** (Iteration Effect): test early to expose errors; match exam style, level, cues; feedback only after genuine effort; rehearse under matched intensity.
7. **Revise:** interleave topics; return at ~20–30% forgetting (spacing).
8. **Recover:** sleep (consolidation — Diekelmann & Born 2010); the H2F lesson — physical recovery architecture supports cognitive training.
9. **Diagnose** (Broken Barrel): every 3–4 months, find the weakest of the five dimensions and fix it first.
