# The art of memory and ancient Egypt — what the documents actually show

**Question:** did the art of memory come from ancient Egypt?

## Short answer

The *documented* trail starts in Greece, not Egypt. But Egypt looms large in how the tradition understood itself — and there are two genuine Egyptian threads in the record.

## 1. The documented origin: Greece → Rome

- **Simonides of Ceos** (c. 556–468 BC): the banquet-hall origin legend, via Cicero (*De Oratore* 2.86) and Quintilian (*Institutio* XI.2) — who notes the ancients themselves disagreed on the details.
- **Earliest surviving technical fragment:** the *Dissoi logoi* / *Dialexis* (c. 400 BC) — a Greek text with memory precepts.
- **Fullest ancient treatment:** *Rhetorica ad Herennium* (c. 86–82 BC, Rome) — ordered *loci* and striking *imagines agentes*.
- No verified pharaonic-Egyptian memory manual exists. Claims of a direct Egyptian origin for the loci system are speculation.

## 2. The two genuine Egyptian threads

**Thread A — Plato's *Phaedrus* (c. 370 BC), a real ancient document with Egyptian framing.**
The Egyptian god **Theuth (Thoth)** invents writing and presents it to King Thamus as "an elixir of memory and wisdom." Thamus replies that it will produce forgetfulness: writing is "a recipe not for memory, but for reminder." This is the earliest philosophical confrontation between natural memory and external memory aids — and it is set in Egypt, deliberately. Anyone tracing "the art of memory" as a *system of thought about memory* (not just technique) starts here as much as with Simonides.

**Thread B — Renaissance Hermeticism, via Yates.**
Marsilio Ficino, Giordano Bruno, John Dee and others believed the *Corpus Hermeticum* preserved the wisdom of ancient Egyptian priests (*prisca theologia*) — and Yates shows this belief shaped the Renaissance art of memory itself, especially Bruno's systems (*The Art of Memory*; *Giordano Bruno and the Hermetic Tradition*, 1964). Modern scholarship (Casaubon 1614; Festugière): the Hermetic texts are **Hellenistic** (2nd–3rd c. AD), products of Greco-Egyptian Alexandria — Egyptian in setting and symbolism, not pharaonic in origin. So: the *tradition's self-image* is Egyptian; the documents are not.

## 3. Why the instinct is right anyway

The art of memory was never a standalone trick — it was always a small part of a larger system, exactly as suspected:

- **Classical:** *memoria* was the fourth of the **five canons of rhetoric** (inventio, dispositio, elocutio, memoria, actio) — one component of the orator's art.
- **Medieval:** memory as part of **Prudence** (Aquinas, *Summa* II-II q.49) — one component of the virtuous life.
- **Renaissance:** Llull's wheels model divine attributes; Camillo's Theatre models the universe; Bruno's system models a Hermetic cosmos — the memory system *is* the world-model.
